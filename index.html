Scan. Verify. Pay.

A dedicated palm terminal authenticates an eligible payment, while UPI remains the payment rail.

PALM→VERIFY→UPI→PAID
01 — The concept

Not a new wallet. A new way to authenticate a payment.

The proposal is to add palm authentication as an additional interface for eligible UPI transactions, rather than creating another payment network.

01

Existing UPI foundation

The user's existing bank account and UPI relationship remain the foundation of the payment.

02

Phone-free checkout

At participating merchants, the user can authenticate through a palm terminal instead of reaching for a phone.

03

Controlled first

Start with a small pilot in high-frequency environments before considering broader deployment.

02 — User experience

One enrollment. A simple gesture at checkout.

STEP 1

Enroll

Register a palm and connect it to the user's eligible payment account.

STEP 2

Scan

The terminal performs palm recognition and liveness checks.

STEP 3

Confirm

The amount is shown clearly and the user authorizes the transaction.

STEP 4

Pay

The transaction is processed through the appropriate UPI/payment infrastructure.

03 — Proposed architecture

Biometrics authenticate. UPI moves the money.

The design goal is to minimize raw biometric exposure, use liveness/anti-spoofing, and keep transaction authorization separate from the biometric template.

Palm terminalCapture + liveness
Biometric layerProtected template + match
PhonePe / PSPConsent + risk checks
UPI / issuerTransaction authorization
04 — Where to test

Don't fight QR everywhere. Test where speed matters.

University / corporate campus

Controlled enrollment, high repeat usage, cafeterias, vending and campus stores.

Airport

High-throughput retail, food and lounge transactions where phone-free convenience has a clear use case.

Mall / food court

Multiple merchants in one environment make adoption and checkout behavior measurable.

Enterprise services

Potential future use across cafeterias and other controlled-access services.

05 — Proposed pilot

A 90-day experiment before a national rollout.

The objective is not to prove that palm payments look futuristic. It is to determine whether customers actually choose them when QR payments are already available.

50–100 terminalsOne or two controlled venues.
90 daysBuild, launch, measure and review.
Key metricsSpeed, adoption, reliability, fraud, cost and repeat use.
06 — Trust by design

Biometrics only work if people trust them.

Explicit consent

Clear enrollment, purpose disclosure and a straightforward opt-out path.

Protected biometric data

Minimize raw image retention and use appropriate template protection and encryption.

Fallback authentication

Palm should be an additional payment interface, with existing payment methods available when it fails.

This is a concept proposal, not a claim that the proposed architecture is already certified or approved for UPI. Any implementation would require review by PhonePe, participating banks, NPCI and relevant regulators/security teams.
07 — Let's test it

Could Indians choose their palm when QR is already available?

This proposal is seeking a conversation with payment, product, offline-payments and technology teams about a small controlled pilot.
